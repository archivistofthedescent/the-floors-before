There's a thing that happens to a party around the third floor, if it gets that far. I've watched it happen more times than I can count, and I have counted. The panic burns off and something stranger replaces it. It isn't courage, exactly. It's *routine*. You stop being five strangers who woke up in the same nightmare. You become a small, filthy household with its own jokes and its own chores, and its own arguments about whose turn it is to go first through a door.

Here were our chores.

Dask went first through doors, because he was the biggest and because he enjoyed it. Hollan went second, because he knew what he was doing. The sisters went third, back to back, cleavers out, Pell muttering and Wim humming. I went last, with the satchel.

And every morning, in whatever safe room we'd found, I took stock.

That's what Orrel called it. Every morning before she opened the shop, she'd walk the shelves and touch every jar. *Stock on a shelf*, she'd say. *If you don't know what you've got, you don't have it.* So every morning, in the dungeon, I walked my shelves.

Dask: left shoulder stiff, sleeping fine, eating like three men. Hollan: knee bad on stairs, hands steady except in narrow corridors, still holding that damn cup. Pell: not sleeping. Pell was never sleeping. Wim: eating too little, laughing too much, a cough she pretended wasn't there.

I kept the list in my head, and I checked it against them every morning, and they hated it.

"Stop *looking* at me like that," Pell would snap.

"Like what?"

"Like I'm a jar."

I brewed in every safe room we found. I used whatever we'd scraped together: weeds off the walls, glands out of the things Hollan killed, the dregs of every loot-box potion we didn't trust. I trusted none of them. Every potion the dungeon gave us came in those gorgeous sunset colours, and every time I tested one, there was something wrong with it. Not poison exactly. A *twist*. Something that healed the wound and borrowed something back. One made you heal faster and dream about drowning. Another closed cuts beautifully, then made you hungry for things that weren't food for about an hour afterwards.

The dungeon doesn't give you anything for free. It wants you to come back for more.

So I made my own, and they were all the colour of weak tea.

They did not always go well.

On the third floor I tried to make a stamina tonic out of a mushroom that grew near the stairs, and a bladder from something we called a cave pig. That was a generous name. It had a face like a shovel and the temperament of a tax collector. I'd boiled it slowly, kept my promise to the heat, done everything right. I gave the first cup to Hollan because his knee was bad.

He turned blue.

Not a little blue. Not *pale*. A rich, deep, royal blue, from his scalp to his toenails, like he'd been dipped in a dye vat. His teeth stayed white. That made it worse.

> **Status Effect Applied: Extremely Blue.**
>
> **Duration: 24 hours. Effects: None. You're just blue. Sorry, but also: this is the most-clipped moment of your season so far.**

There was a long silence in the safe room.

Then Hollan laughed.

I'd never heard him laugh before. None of us had. It came out of him like something breaking loose. He laughed until he had to sit down, and then he laughed sitting down, blue and helpless, holding his cup. Wim started laughing too, and then apologising for laughing, and then laughing harder. Dask was slapping his knee so hard I worried about the shoulder. Even Pell put her hand over her mouth and turned away. Her shoulders shook.

"My knee," Hollan finally gasped, "my knee feels *great*."

It did, too. The tonic worked perfectly. The blue was just a side effect.

I wrote that down in my head: cave pig bladder, excellent for joints, unacceptable for dignity. I've used it since. I always warn people about the blue. They never listen, and they always turn blue.

I think that potion did more for Hollan than anything else I made him, before or after. Not because of the knee. Because for one day he was ridiculous and alive, and he remembered he could be both.

The fourth floor was a city.

Not a real one. The dungeon built it out of pieces of cities it had already eaten, so the streets didn't go anywhere sensible. A cobbled lane would turn into a marble staircase, which turned into a wooden pier over nothing at all. Every building had a door, and every door was a decision. Some led to loot. Some led to monsters. Some led to rooms full of very comfortable furniture, and if you sat down you never got up again.

We'd been there four days. We were close to the stairwell. We knew we were close, because the monsters were getting worse, and the dungeon always gets worse right before it lets you go.

The last street before the stairwell was a long, narrow corridor of iron doors. Each one hung in a stone arch, slamming shut on a rhythm, one after another, like teeth closing. The sign over the entrance said **THE GAUNTLET OF HASTE** in cheerful gold letters. Underneath, in smaller letters: **sponsored by a Valued Partner.**

"Timing," said Hollan. "We wait for the rhythm. We go one at a time."

"The rhythm's not a rhythm," I said. I'd been watching it for ten minutes. "It changes. It speeds up when someone's in it."

"Then we go fast."

We went fast. Hollan went first, through the doors like a man who'd done this in a war, and he probably had. Then Pell, dragging Wim. Then me, with my satchel banging against my hip, my heart going so hard I couldn't hear. And then there was only one door left between me and the others, and it was closing. I'd misjudged it by half a step.

Dask was behind me. Dask was always behind me now, in the bad places. He'd started doing that after the sting, without ever saying anything about it.

He put his hand on my back and shoved.

I went through the gap. I went through so hard that I hit the far wall and saw stars. When I turned around, the door was closed and Dask was on the other side of it. Through the little iron grille at head height I could see his face, his grey beard and his enormous sunburnt grin.

"Useful," he said through the grille. "Stay useful, boy."

Then the doors behind him started closing too, faster and faster, all of them at once, like the whole corridor was clenching its jaw.

I didn't open it for three days.

---

I want to tell you that I raged. That I swore revenge, smashed the grille with my pestle, and stood in that corridor screaming at the cameras. That's what the viewers like. I've seen a lot of crawlers do it since, and I don't blame any of them.

I didn't do that.

I sat down against the wall, and I took stock.

Hollan: unhurt. Hands shaking. Still holding the cup. Pell: unhurt. Face white. Already turning toward the stairwell, because somebody had to. Wim: unhurt. Not laughing. Not humming. Not doing anything at all, just staring at the closed door with her round face completely empty.

I was the one who knew what was wrong with everybody, so I knew what was wrong with all of them. I didn't know what was wrong with me. I couldn't find it on the shelf.

I didn't stop brewing after that. If anything I brewed more. Every safe room, all night, the little brass burner going and the weak-tea colours coming out right every time. The others slept, or pretended to.

I just stopped talking while I did it.
